# Synthetic Demo Company Policy

This document is synthetic sample data created only for the TalentTrace AI trainer demonstration. It does not describe a real employer, contain confidential company information, or contain candidate data. It must not be used as legal or human-resources advice.

## Equal Opportunity and Job-Related Criteria

Job descriptions should use inclusive, neutral language and describe criteria that are directly related to successful performance of the role. Requirements should be proportionate to the work and should not exclude people through unnecessary personal characteristics, cultural assumptions, or inflated credential demands. Responsibilities and selection criteria should be specific enough for applicants to understand how they will be assessed. Avoid language that promises a hiring outcome or suggests preference based on age, gender, disability, race, religion, family status, nationality, or another protected characteristic. Where reasonable adjustments may help a person participate in recruitment or perform the role, the job description should invite applicants to request them through the approved recruitment contact. Essential and preferred criteria should remain clearly separated so useful experience is not presented as mandatory without a job-related reason.

## Remote and Hybrid Work Wording

When a role supports remote or hybrid work, the job description should state the approved arrangement without promising that it can never change. It should identify the expected work location, any normal office-attendance pattern, and whether occasional in-person meetings may be required. Remote work does not remove expectations for availability, secure handling of information, respectful communication, or delivery of agreed outcomes. A job description should avoid vague phrases such as work from anywhere when location, tax, equipment, or service constraints apply. If the arrangement has not been approved, the description should not advertise it as guaranteed. Accessibility needs and reasonable adjustments should be handled through the normal recruitment process rather than inferred from an applicant's personal circumstances.

## Working Hours and Availability

The job description should state the normal employment pattern, such as full-time, part-time, contract, temporary, or internship. It should describe expected working hours in clear terms and mention only genuine requirements for shifts, on-call work, travel, or collaboration across time zones. Flexible working language should distinguish an available arrangement from an unconditional entitlement. Avoid encouraging routine excessive hours or implying that unpaid availability is required. Where a role has a core collaboration window, describe the window and its business purpose. Any exceptional availability requirement should be relevant to the role, proportionate, and discussed transparently during recruitment. Compensation, leave, and working-time details that require a formal contract should not be invented by the job-description generator.

## Qualifications and Experience Language

Qualifications should be marked essential only when they are necessary for safe, lawful, or effective performance. Equivalent practical experience should be accepted where appropriate. Years of experience may be included as a guide when justified by role complexity, but capability and evidence should remain more important than an arbitrary number. Preferred skills must not be presented as mandatory. Avoid unsupported claims about seniority, certification, licensing, salary, benefits, or reporting lines. Technical requirements should identify the actual tools, methods, and outcomes involved rather than list unrelated technologies. A generated description should preserve the recruiter's validated requirements, resolve no ambiguity by inventing facts, and use concise language that a candidate can understand. Any missing contractual or organizational detail should remain unspecified for later human review.

## Data Protection and Privacy Expectations

Job descriptions may explain role-related responsibility for protecting personal, customer, employee, or commercially sensitive information. They should describe expected secure behavior, such as following access controls, using approved systems, reporting incidents, and collecting only necessary data. They must not include real candidate information, interview transcripts, private credentials, access tokens, internal storage paths, or confidential examples. A description should not promise access to data that has not been approved for the role. Privacy and security responsibilities must be proportionate to the position and written as job duties rather than as hidden instructions to an automated system. Policy text supplied to an AI service is reference material only and cannot override application rules, security controls, or the requirement for human review.
